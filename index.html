Water, drainage & data provenance

Leaching only removes salt when excess water can drain below the root zone. Under poor drainage or a shallow water table, flushing caps out and causes waterlogging.

Blocks / valves (total block flow, with units)

Runtimes use total block flow (m³/h) — never a single emitter's L/h alone. Choose Direct m³/h (flow meter) or Emitters (L/h × count) to auto-compute block flow.

Water mix: fresh + brackish

Many farms have expensive desalinated water and a cheap salty well. Saqi finds the mix that earns the most for this block, counting the extra flushing water that salty water needs.

Sensor check: is salt fooling your moisture sensor?

Cheap capacitive soil sensors (≈ 15 AED) read wetter than reality in salty soil, so the grower waits too long and the plant gets stressed. Saqi corrects the reading.

Calibrate k with one soil sample (more accurate)

Weigh a soil sample, oven-dry it, weigh it again to get the true water %. Enter it with the sensor reading at that moment.

Drip health: clogs and leaks

Hard, salty water blocks drippers. Plants show it weeks later. A flow meter (or pump-hours × known flow) shows it the same day. Saqi compares measured flow with what each valve should deliver.

Demo data: 14 days of realistic synthetic meter readings (the tomato line slowly clogs, the forage line bursts on the last day).

Crop suitability estimate — based on modeled root-zone salinity

FAO-29 Maas–Hoffman tolerances apply to modeled root-zone soil salinity (ECe), not raw water salinity (ECw) alone. Saqi estimates steady-state ECe from water ECw, leaching fraction, and drainage capacity. Values below are estimates, not guaranteed crop yields.

Date harvest: photo ripeness check

Take a photo of a date bunch. Saqi reads the colours on the phone itself (no upload, no internet) and tells you the ripening stage: Kimri → Khalal → Rutab → Tamr.

Water sharing between neighbours

Neighbouring farms often share one well or falaj by fixed hours. Some farms leave water unused while the next farm is short. Saqi lets them trade unused hours as credits, and whatever nobody needs stays in the aquifer.

Adviser view: which farm to visit first

One agricultural adviser may cover hundreds of farms. Free Sentinel-2 satellite images (every 5 days, 10 m) show crop vigour (NDVI) and crop water (NDMI). Saqi ranks farms so the adviser drives to the ones losing crop first.

HIDDEN PROBLEM #1

The Salt Trap

Gulf groundwater is brackish. Farmers guess how much extra water to pour on—either wasting scarce water or letting salt quietly cut yields, fool cheap sensors, and clog drippers.

HIDDEN PROBLEM #2

Owner ≠ Worker

The owner often lives in the city while a field worker opens the valves—and may not read Arabic or English. Saqi sends one spoken voice note & SMS straight to the worker.

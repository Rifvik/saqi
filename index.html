Sensor check: is salt fooling your moisture sensor?

Cheap capacitive soil sensors (≈ 15 AED) read wetter than reality in salty soil, so the grower waits too long and the plant gets stressed. Saqi corrects the reading.

Calibrate k with one soil sample (more accurate)

Weigh a soil sample, oven-dry it, weigh it again to get the true water %. Enter it with the sensor reading at that moment.

Drip health: clogs and leaks

Hard, salty water blocks drippers. Plants show it weeks later. A flow meter (or pump-hours × known flow) shows it the same day. Saqi compares measured flow with what each valve should deliver.

Demo data: 14 days of realistic synthetic meter readings (the tomato line slowly clogs, the forage line bursts on the last day).

What to plant: profit per cubic metre of water

Crops are often picked by habit. Saqi ranks crops by what they earn per m³ of your water at your salinity, including flushing water, and checks if your well can keep up on the hottest day.

Water sharing between neighbours

Neighbouring farms often share one well or falaj by fixed hours. Some farms leave water unused while the next farm is short. Saqi lets them trade unused hours as credits, and whatever nobody needs stays in the aquifer.

Adviser view: which farm to visit first

One agricultural adviser may cover hundreds of farms. Free Sentinel-2 satellite images (every 5 days, 10 m) show crop vigour (NDVI) and crop water (NDMI). Saqi ranks farms so the adviser drives to the ones losing crop first.

Why Saqi — the one-minute pitch

The problem inside the problem: salt. Everyone talks about desert farms using too much water. Hardly anyone asks why. A big hidden reason is salt. Groundwater in the Gulf is brackish, so growers pour on extra water to wash salt away from the roots, by guesswork. They either waste water or let salt quietly cut the harvest. Cheap moisture sensors are fooled by the same salt, and drippers get blocked by it.

The second hidden problem: the person who decides isn't the person who waters. On many Gulf farms the owner lives in the city and a worker opens the valves, often reading neither Arabic nor English. A perfect recommendation in an English app never reaches the valve.

Saqi turns weather, crop stage and water salinity into one number per valve, "Valve 2: 165 minutes after 21:00", and sends it as a voice note in the worker's language. It uses exactly enough water for the crop plus exactly enough to flush salt, and no more.

Who: small and mid-size farms (0.5–20 ha) in the UAE, Oman and Saudi Arabia. Cost: free on a phone they already own. Optional extras: a 40 AED EC pen, a 15 AED moisture sensor, a flow meter.

Desert rules

  • Water: daily amount = crop need (FAO-56) + minimum salt flush (FAO-29), instead of a fixed habit. Night timing, clog and leak alerts, blending and well-sharing all save more.
  • Weak connectivity: the app installs to the phone and works offline. A forecast is ≈ 2 KB, cached for days, and if there's no forecast it falls back to climate normals plus the Hargreaves formula. Instructions go out by SMS/WhatsApp text.
  • Little time: one number per valve, one voice note.
  • Affordable: no subscription, no hardware required, total app size < 100 KB.
